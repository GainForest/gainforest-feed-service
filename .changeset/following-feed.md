Add the following feed, `app.gainforest.feed.defs#followingFeed`, to `getFeed` and `getFeedSkeleton`. It holds what the accounts a viewer follows have published, as the same `recordItem`, `repostItem`, and `groupItem` cards, with the same groups, `limit`, row budget, and version 3 cursor as the activity feed. The Lexicon change is additive: the `followingFeed` token, `followingFeedParams` (`filter`, as for the activity feed, and an optional `viewerDid`), and both added to the `feedId` known values and the `params` union of both procedures. `#activityFeed` and its params are unchanged, and it still gives every caller the same page.

The viewer is the verified caller of an authenticated request: `params` and `viewerDid` may be left out, and a `viewerDid` that names anyone else is rejected with `InvalidRequest`. An anonymous request must send `followingFeedParams` with a `viewerDid` (`InvalidRequest` otherwise); follow records and the records they lead to are public, so anyone can ask for any account's following feed. A cursor belongs to the feed that issued it: each feed rejects the other's with `InvalidCursor`.

The viewer follows the subjects of their valid `app.certified.graph.follow` records. Projects, observations, organizations, and posts are in the feed when a followed account stored them, a repost when a followed account reposted, and a recording when a followed account owns its recorder folder: a recording another account stored in a folder a followed account owns is included (while the folder's `app.gainforest.ac.deployment` record exists and is valid), and a recording a followed account stored in a folder someone else owns is not. A followed account's own recordings, without a folder or in its own folder, are in the feed as for the activity feed. A viewer who follows nobody gets an empty page and the service reads no collection for it. A viewer who follows more than 500 accounts gets the 500 most recently followed (an account followed twice counts once), which bounds what a statement reads for the followed accounts. Reading the viewer's follow records themselves is linear in how many there are (about 100 ms per statement at 30,000 follow records, 200 ms at 100,000).

The statement starts from the followed accounts and reads each account's records through `idx_record_timeline_author_collection_created`, so its cost depends on how many accounts the viewer follows and not on the size of the collections. Recordings are the exception, because Hyperindex has no index on `deploymentRef`: recordings that other accounts stored in a followed account's folder are found by walking the audio collection, only for a viewer whose follows own a folder, and a followed account's own recordings are read in full on every statement, about 2 to 4 microseconds each. That is nothing for an account with hundreds and noticeable for one with tens of thousands, an organization with a large archive of its own recordings included. `docs/database-contract.md` has the measurements and an index proposal for the Hyperindex owners. No migration or index change is needed. The `feed` series of the `hypercerts_feed_database_duration_seconds` histogram now carries a `feed` label (`activity` or `following`), so the two feeds' latencies can be told apart. `scripts/test-hyperindex-schema.sh` now also requires `idx_record_timeline_author_collection_created` (migration 010).
